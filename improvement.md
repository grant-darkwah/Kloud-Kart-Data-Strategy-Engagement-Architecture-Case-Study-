## Improvement beyond the brief: Customer Data Register and Deletion Drill

### The problem it solves

The brief asks for a reply to the Commission, but the reply needs evidence. Customer details sit in at least six kinds of places (laptops, email, WhatsApp or shared drives, Marketing’s tool, backups, delivery and payment partners), and nobody has a list. The roadmap’s “list of all places” (due Fri 4 Dec 2026) has no standing format. Without one, the list goes stale, and the next deletion request starts from scratch.

### What I built

One shared register, kept as a simple sheet, with one row per place customer data lives:

It comes with two companion pieces:

- **A 5-step deletion drill:** find the customer’s code, check every register row, remove, record the date and person, and file a one-page proof.
- **A reply skeleton for the Commission:** what we found, where, what we removed and when, and what now stops it happening again.

The register feeds the Quarter 1 deliverables (the dated list by 4 Dec 2026 and the answer by 15 Jan 2027). It is reused unchanged for the Quarter 3 deletion test (25 Jun 2027) and for Nigeria. Its first version is filled in from the staff lists already asked for in the first two weeks.

### What it is worth to Kloud Kart

- **A reply that holds up.** The Commission answer rests on a dated record, not on memory.
- **Deletions that repeat.** Any later request takes one pass through the register, not a new hunt.
- **A board answer.** “Where is customer data, and who owns it?” has a one-page reply.
- **Nigeria readiness.** The register shows where Nigerian data would land before collection starts.
